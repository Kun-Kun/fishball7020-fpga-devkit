Program the ADF4159 ramp and capture the TX1 → RX1 or TX2 → RX2 beat signal.

FMCW ramp

The VCO sweep must stay within 4–8 GHz, so the largest span depends on its start frequency. Both external AD9361 LOs must receive the VCO output; their RF LO is half its frequency. The sweep leg is the up ramp; a full triangle takes twice as long.

Edit the fields to calculate the quantized ramp.

Single sweep: connect ADF4159 MUXOUT to JP5 pin 14 (FPGA H17). TXDATA on JP5 pin 4 starts one ramp. MUXOUT reports ramp completion; the FPGA closes the RX gates and latches done until the next trigger. Trigger once waits for completion and does not capture samples.

Ramp gate status has not been checked.

Ideal FMCW relations: VCO hop = (fPFD/2²⁵) × DEV × 2DEV_OFFSET; hop time = CLK1 × CLK2 / fPFD. RF slope S = BRF/T; beat fb ≈ Sτ, with τ = 2R/c for a reflected target. Range resolution ΔR = c/(2BRF). One chirp's FFT bin spacing is 1/T. Values are theoretical; PLL tracking, coax velocity and analog bandwidth affect measurements.

Live FMCW beat

Capture one sweep or stream repeated single sawtooth FFTs. TXDATA starts each sweep, MUXOUT ends it, and both TX outputs mute after every sweep.

TX guard status has not been checked.

The FFT window is applied after skipping the selected samples. Hann is the previous default; Blackman–Harris suppresses distant spectral leakage more strongly but widens peaks. Skipping samples can remove startup transients, but also shortens the observation and reduces frequency resolution. Up to one quarter of a chirp may be skipped.

TX1 DC uses I = 16000 by default and Q = 0; its RF carrier follows the chirped external TX LO. RX1 mixes the delayed signal with the chirped RX LO, so no FPGA complex multiplier is needed. TX2 retains its existing FPGA dechirp path. The TX2 tone frequency is an offset from the external LO (VCO frequency ÷ 2). Enabling records your check of the selected TX port; it does not transmit until Capture beat or Start live is pressed. The affirmation expires at board reboot. Capture supports 0.5–2 ms sweep legs and accepts RX samples only while the FPGA gate is active. Live streaming requires Single sawtooth and honors the repeat period when setup and capture finish in time. Closing the stream stops further sweeps. A single 1 ms FFT has roughly 1 kHz bin spacing and cannot resolve a tens-of-hertz beat from a short path.

Zero distance is at the top; negative FFT bins run downward.
