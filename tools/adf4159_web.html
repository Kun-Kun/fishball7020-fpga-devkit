Program the ADF4159 ramp and capture the TX1 → RX1 or TX2 → RX2 beat signal.

FMCW ramp

The VCO sweep must stay within 4–8 GHz, so the largest span depends on its start frequency. Both external AD9361 LOs must receive the VCO output; their RF LO is half its frequency. The sweep leg is the up ramp; a full triangle takes twice as long.

Edit the fields to calculate the quantized ramp.

Single sweep: connect ADF4159 MUXOUT to JP5 pin 14 (FPGA H17). TXDATA on JP5 pin 4 starts one ramp. MUXOUT reports ramp completion; the FPGA closes the RX gates and latches done until the next trigger. Trigger once waits for completion and does not capture samples.

Ramp gate status has not been checked.

Ideal FMCW relations: VCO hop = (fPFD/2²⁵) × DEV × 2DEV_OFFSET; hop time = CLK1 × CLK2 / fPFD. RF slope S = BRF/T; beat fb ≈ Sτ, with τ = 2R/c for a reflected target. Range resolution ΔR = c/(2BRF). One chirp's FFT bin spacing is 1/T. Values are theoretical; PLL tracking, coax velocity and analog bandwidth affect measurements.

Live FMCW beat

Capture one sweep or stream repeated single sawtooth FFTs. TXDATA starts each sweep, MUXOUT ends it, and both TX outputs mute after every sweep.

TX guard status has not been checked.

The FFT window is applied after skipping the selected samples. Hann is the previous default; Blackman–Harris suppresses distant spectral leakage more strongly but widens peaks. Skipping samples can remove startup transients, but also shortens the observation and reduces frequency resolution. Up to one quarter of a chirp may be skipped. Live frames keep native FFT bins within ±100 kHz and a compact full-band overview; one-shot capture retains the full FFT.

Choose one transmit and receive polarization, or a TX1/TX2 → RX1 + RX2 path to capture both receivers on the same TXDATA trigger. Dual RX requires Single sawtooth; Capture beat shows separate RX1 and RX2 time traces and FFTs. Display receiver selects the live FFT, waterfall and CSV download. DC mode uses I = 16000 and Q = 0; the RF carrier follows the swept external TX LO. For a tone or chirp on either TX, both RX multipliers use that selected TX reference. The tone frequency is an offset from the external LO (VCO frequency ÷ 2). Enabling records your check of the selected TX port; it does not transmit until Capture beat or Start live is pressed. The affirmation expires at board reboot. Capture supports 0.4–10 ms sweep legs and uses start/end markers to recover the actual sample count across DMA buffers. Live streaming requires Single sawtooth and honors the repeat period when setup and capture finish in time. Closing the stream stops further sweeps.

One four-bit digital shift multiplies signed I/Q samples on both RX paths by 2^N before DMA and saturates at the 16-bit limits. Zero leaves samples unchanged. Large shifts can clip. DC captures use the raw external-LO beat on both RX channels; tone/chirp captures use the shared TX reference and both FPGA multipliers.

Polarimetric comparison · R/L transmit, V/H receive

Capture TX1 (R) with RX1 (V) and RX2 (H), then TX2 (L) with both receivers. The same ramp, TX waveform, attenuation, RX gain, digital shift and FFT settings above apply to both sweeps.

Each waterfall column is one four-beat set. Blue means negative difference, white is 0 dB, and red means positive difference; black means one or both returns fell below the power floor. The horizontal axis is elapsed time; range increases downward. Start polarimetric live repeats sets using the repeat period above, or as fast as capture permits. Stop finishes the current set and prevents the next one.

The power average uses adjacent FFT bins. Differences are shown only where both compared returns exceed the floor. Range uses ideal air round-trip delay and the programmed chirp slope; antenna paths and polarization-channel gains are not calibrated. Sequential TX sweeps can observe a moving target at different times. These power contrasts are not standard weather-radar ZDR, phase or correlation products.

The color is relative power: black is at least 50 dB below the brightest cell, and bright peach is the maximum. RX2 right makes positive angles point toward RX2. The phase offset compensates a fixed RX cable/receiver mismatch; measure it with a known broadside target. At 260 mm and about 2.25 GHz, spacing is about 1.95 wavelengths: multiple angles have the same RX phase. Bright lobes are ambiguous. With only two receivers, a denser spatial FFT interpolates the response but does not resolve multiple targets at the same range.
